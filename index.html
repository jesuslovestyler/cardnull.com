Cardnull OS

Cardnull OS is in active development now: a whole computer system built from the ground up — including its own kernel, written in Swift from scratch and released into the public domain, like all of Cardnull’s work. On November 6, 2026 it reached its first five milestones, each one verified booting and running on Tyler’s own Mac: it boots and writes CARDNULL on the screen; it takes keyboard input; it answers commands in a small shell (help, about, verse, clear); it runs on interrupts with a 100 Hz timer heartbeat and an uptime command; and it boots through a UEFI door as well as the classic BIOS door — one system, two doors. It is still in development — not released, and there is no public download yet.

An operating system that starts from the faith

Our own kernel is written from scratch, in Swift — the heart of the system is ours at every layer, and public domain like the rest of our work. TempleOS, Terry Davis’s from-scratch, public-domain system built for God, is our precedent and our teacher — never our foundation. On that heart we will build a calm, simple Christian system: the browser is Chirpy, the Bible is on the shelf from the first boot, and updates are easy enough for a church secretary or a grandmother to run.

It will be a free download, made to give older machines in church offices and homeschool rooms a second life.

About Cardnull

Cardnull is the work of Tyler Kovalevich, a Christian missionary and former research chemist in eastern North Carolina — in his own words, “a public domain programmer and missionary trying to get the gospel to the ends of the earth.”

Everything here is given away on purpose. The code is public domain, the books are free, and the browser belongs to whoever downloads it. Cardnull exists so that Christian families, churches, and schools can have good tools without paying for them — or being tracked by them.
